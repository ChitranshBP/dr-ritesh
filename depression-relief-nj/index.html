Yes. TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) is FDA-approved and has been used clinically for over a decade. It's non-invasive and drug-free, requires no sedation, and has no systemic side effects. The most common complaint is mild scalp discomfort during the first few sessions, which usually settles quickly.

Most major plans — including Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Horizon BCBS, Oxford, Medicare and Amerihealth — cover TMS and Spravato® for treatment-resistant depression when criteria are met. We verify your specific benefits for free before you start, so you know your out-of-pocket cost in advance.

TMS sessions run about 20 minutes with no recovery time — most patients return straight to work. Spravato® visits take roughly two hours including the required observation period. Ketamine infusions typically run 40–60 minutes.

It varies. Spravato® and ketamine often produce a shift within days — sometimes hours. With TMS, most patients notice change somewhere between weeks two and four of a standard course. Approximately 70% of TMS patients experience significant improvement in their depressive symptoms, and many reach remission.
